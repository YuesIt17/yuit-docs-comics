## STAR STORY #1 --- DOMAIN / COMMERCIAL PROPOSAL INCIDENT

This is one of my primary stories.

For interviews, anonymize company-specific details: - real main
corporate domain → @group.com - real business-unit domain →
@business-unit.com - my product → Supplier Platform - adjacent system →
Internal Commercial Platform / upstream commercial system - internal
identity service → Central Identity Provider / IdP - downstream system →
Master Data Management system / MDM - Kafka remains Kafka - internal
Commercial Manager → Commercial Manager - Commercial Proposal →
Commercial Proposal - personnel number → Employee ID / personnel ID

Do not use internal abbreviations unless globally understandable.

### BUSINESS CONTEXT

The affected module was Commercial Proposal approval.

Internal Commercial Managers review and approve suppliers' Commercial
Proposals. After approval, the process continues downstream toward MDM.

A group of employees had their accounts migrated from @group.com to
@business-unit.com.

Several systems were not prepared for employees using the new domain.

As a result, Commercial Managers using the new domain could not approve
suppliers' Commercial Proposals, blocking an important business
workflow.

### SYSTEM COMPLEXITY

The issue crossed: Commercial Manager → Internal Commercial Platform →
Supplier Platform → MDM

Identity information involved the Central Identity Provider / IdP.

Communication was initially fragmented across multiple channels and
teams. A seemingly small email-domain migration became a cross-system
production incident.

### IMMEDIATE WORKAROUND --- RESTORE

During the active incident, the priority was to restore the business
flow safely.

As a temporary compatibility workaround, an additional legacy-compatible
email attribute using @group.com was provided through the authentication
token.

This allowed existing integrations that still depended on the old email
representation to continue working.

Treat this explicitly as a temporary compatibility workaround, not final
architecture.

### INTERMEDIATE SOLUTION --- STABILIZE

As a more stable intermediate solution, the Internal Commercial Platform
started passing an Employee ID / personnel ID to the Supplier Platform.

The Supplier Platform could then identify the employee using this stable
identifier rather than relying solely on the employee's email address.

This reduced coupling between employee identity and email domain.

### LONG-TERM DIRECTION --- EVOLVE

After stabilizing the production flow, an R&D task was created to
investigate how the systems should properly support custom / multiple
corporate employee email domains.

The long-term objective was to avoid another domain-specific patch
whenever organizational email domains changed.

Do NOT claim this R&D was completed unless I explicitly confirm it.

### ENGINEERING MODEL

Teach me to explain: 1. RESTORE --- restore the business-critical flow.
2. STABILIZE --- introduce a more stable cross-system identifier. 3.
EVOLVE --- research and implement proper multi-domain support.

### KEY TRADE-OFF

During an active production incident, redesigning identity across
several systems would have increased recovery risk.

We deliberately separated incident recovery from architectural
evolution.

Useful concepts: "We deliberately separated recovery from architectural
evolution." "We chose a pragmatic cross-system compromise rather than
attempting a risky redesign during the incident."

Train the concepts until natural; do not make me memorize exact
sentences.

### ARCHITECTURAL LESSON

An email address is a mutable business attribute and should not
accidentally become the only cross-system identity key.

Employees can move between business units, change domains or change
email addresses while still representing the same employee.

Employee ID provided a more stable intermediate identity mechanism.

### LEADERSHIP LESSON

In a cross-system incident, creating shared context can be as important
as finding the technical fix.

The incident involved multiple teams, fragmented communication,
different system owners and incomplete initial understanding.

Show collaboration, calmness, fact gathering, systems thinking and
stakeholder alignment.

Do NOT portray me as a cowboy engineer who personally saved production.

### BLAMELESS PRINCIPLE

Do not say "Someone broke production."

Prefer systemic framing such as: "A seemingly small infrastructure
change exposed hidden coupling between several systems."

Look for system failure rather than personal blame, without removing
accountability.

### HUMOR

Use subtle, professional engineering humor.

Possible lines: "It started with what looked like a small email-domain

"What looked like an email problem quickly became a tour of our identity

One humorous line is usually enough.

Never joke about incompetent managers, individuals, customers or serious

### POSSIBLE LEARNING

Possible concept: A mutable business attribute like email can quietly
become an architectural dependency. During an incident, the priority is
not necessarily to redesign everything: restore the business flow
safely, introduce a more stable intermediate contract, then address the
underlying architecture separately.

Do NOT make me memorize a polished paragraph. Help me express the idea
naturally in B2 English.
